## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 About this plan

This is the proposed local plan for Northwold District. When adopted it will set out where new homes, jobs and infrastructure will go in the district between 2027 and 2042, and the policies the Council will use to decide planning applications. The plan period is 15 years, from 1 April 2027 to 31 March 2042. We expect to adopt the plan in spring 2029.

The plan has been prepared under the 30-month local plan process introduced by the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 and the Town and Country Planning (Local Planning) (England) Regulations 2026. It follows our Gateway 1 self-assessment, published in October 2026, and two rounds of consultation: the scoping consultation in summer 2026 and the consultation on proposed plan content and evidence in spring 2027.

### 1.2 How this plan fits with other plans

The development plan for Northwold will consist of this local plan, the Wendshire Minerals and Waste Local Plan, and the four made neighbourhood plans for Saxholme, Little Harrow, Coldmarsh and Fenwick St Mary.

There is no spatial development strategy for Northwold at present. The Fenland and Wolds Strategic Authority began preparing one in 2027 and expects to publish a draft in 2029. We have taken its emerging evidence on the housing market area into account, and we will review this plan if the strategy, once adopted, requires more homes in the district.

### 1.3 Policies this plan replaces

On adoption, this plan will replace the Northwold Core Strategy (2014) and the Northwold Site Allocations Plan (2016) in full.

### 1.5 Working with our neighbours

Northwold shares a housing market with Eastmarsh Borough and a transport network with the rest of Wendshire. We meet officers from Eastmarsh Borough Council and Wendshire County Council every quarter. A statement of common ground on housing, transport and water supply is being prepared and will be published before the plan is submitted. Eastmarsh has said it can meet its own housing need, and we have not been asked to meet any of it.

## 2 Northwold today

Northwold is a largely rural district of 520 square kilometres in the west of Wendshire, home to about 118,000 people. Nearly two in five live in Wendbury, the district's main town and its only railway station, on the Fenline to the regional city. The market towns of Coldmarsh and Saxholme serve the south and east of the district, and there are about forty villages, the largest of which is Little Harrow.

The district has three contrasting landscapes. To the north rise the chalk hills of the Northwold Wolds, a nationally designated landscape. The River Wend runs through the middle of the district, with a wide floodplain that has flooded parts of Wendbury and Coldmarsh twice since 2012. To the south lie the flat, drained farmland of the Northwold Fens, among the most productive soils in the country.

Wendbury grew around its medieval minster and a malting industry that closed in the 1990s. The town centre has a fine historic core, protected by a conservation area, but 14% of its shop units were empty in 2025, and the former Maltings has stood vacant for more than twenty years.

Our evidence identifies the main issues the plan must address:

- Homes are expensive. The median house costs 9.8 times median earnings, and 1,950 households are on the housing register.
- The population is ageing. A quarter of residents will be over 65 by 2040.
- Most jobs are in agriculture, food processing and logistics, and wages are below the regional average.
- Flood risk from the River Wend is increasing as the climate changes.
- Most journeys are by car. Rural bus services have been cut, and Wendbury suffers congestion at the Wend Bridge.
- Water supply is under serious stress, and the Wendbury water recycling centre is close to capacity.
- The Council has declared a climate emergency and aims for the district to be net zero by 2040.

## 3 Vision and outcomes

### 3.1 Our vision for Northwold in 2042

By 2042, Northwold will be a district where people of every age can find a home they can afford, close to work, services and green space. Wendbury will have grown to the north, with a new neighbourhood around a primary school and local centre, and its town centre will be busy again, with new homes and life brought back to the Maltings. A new garden village at Harrowfield, on the former airfield, will be well under way, with its own schools, jobs and a frequent bus link to Wendbury station. Coldmarsh and Saxholme will remain thriving market towns, and our villages will grow only to keep their schools, shops and buses going.

The chalk hills of the Northwold Wolds, the River Wend and the Fens will be protected and richer in wildlife. New development will be built to be net zero carbon, will be safe from flooding, and will make it easier to walk, cycle and use public transport.

### 3.2 Aims and objectives

- SO1 Homes: provide enough homes of the right size, type and tenure to meet the needs of all our residents.
- SO2 Economy: support new and growing businesses and better-paid jobs, particularly in food technology and advanced manufacturing.
- SO3 Town centres: bring Wendbury, Coldmarsh and Saxholme town centres back to life.
- SO4 Environment: protect and enhance the Wolds, the Wend valley and the Fens, and help nature recover.
- SO5 Climate: cut carbon emissions from new development and adapt to a changing climate.
- SO6 Infrastructure: make sure schools, health care, roads, buses and water infrastructure keep pace with growth.
- SO7 Communities: support healthy, safe and inclusive communities.

### 3.3 Measurable outcomes

We will measure the plan's success against nine outcomes:

- Outcome 1: At least 9,600 new homes completed between 2027 and 2042, an average of 640 a year.
- Outcome 2: At least 2,800 affordable homes completed by 2042.
- Outcome 3: 32 hectares of new employment land developed and 3,500 net new jobs created by 2042.
- Outcome 4: Shop vacancy in Wendbury town centre below 8% by 2035.
- Outcome 5: A greener Northwold.
- Outcome 6: All new homes net zero carbon in operation from 2030.
- Outcome 7: Better health and wellbeing for our residents.
- Outcome 8: No new homes permitted in Flood Zone 3 against the advice of the Environment Agency.
- Outcome 9: 20% more journeys in Wendbury made on foot, by bicycle or by public transport by 2040, compared with 2025.

## 4 Spatial strategy

### Policy SP1: Spatial strategy and settlement hierarchy

Development will be focused on Wendbury as the district's main town and on a new garden village at Harrowfield, with more limited growth in the market towns and villages, in line with the settlement hierarchy below. Over the plan period, new homes will be distributed broadly as follows:

| Place | Role | Share of new homes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wendbury | Main town | 60% |
| Harrowfield Garden Village | New settlement | 16% |
| Coldmarsh and Saxholme | Market towns | 17% |
| Little Harrow and other villages | Villages | 7% |

Outside the settlement boundaries shown on the policies map, development will be limited to that which needs a countryside location.

#### Reasons for the strategy

Four options for the distribution of growth were tested in the Sustainability Appraisal: concentrating growth in Wendbury; a new settlement; dispersal across the towns and villages; and a combination of Wendbury and a new settlement. The combination was chosen because Wendbury has the only railway station and most of the district's jobs and services, while its capacity to grow beyond 2035 is limited by the Wend floodplain to the south and the Wolds to the north. A new settlement at Harrowfield makes use of a large area of previously developed land at the former airfield and can provide its own schools and services. Dispersal was rejected because it would increase car journeys and could not fund new infrastructure.

### Policy SP2: Housing requirement

Provision will be made for at least 9,600 net additional homes between 2027 and 2042, an average of 640 homes a year. This meets in full the local housing need of 612 homes a year, calculated using the standard method in 2026, with a margin of about 5% to allow for sites that do not come forward.

The Local Housing Needs Assessment (2025) shows a need for 248 affordable homes a year, and that 40% of new homes should have one or two bedrooms. It also identifies a need for 1,100 homes for older people, including 420 extra care homes, over the plan period.

### Policy SP3: Employment land

Provision will be made for at least 32 hectares of new employment land between 2027 and 2042, at the Wend Valley Business Park extension (NW/E1, 18 hectares), at Harrowfield Garden Village (NW/GV1, 8 hectares), and on smaller sites within Wendbury, Coldmarsh and Saxholme (6 hectares). Existing employment areas shown on the policies map will be protected for business use.

### Policy SP4: Infrastructure

Development will be expected to provide, or contribute towards, the infrastructure needed to support it, through planning obligations and the Community Infrastructure Levy. The infrastructure needed to deliver this plan will be set out in the Infrastructure Delivery Plan, which is being prepared with Wendshire County Council, the Wendshire Integrated Care Board and Wendshire Water.

Transport improvements will be guided by the Wendshire Local Transport Plan 4 (2025), which proposes a second crossing of the River Wend at Wendbury and a frequent bus route between Harrowfield and Wendbury station.

### Policy NW/H1: Land north of Wendbury

Site reference: NW/H1. Site area: 42 hectares. Existing use: agricultural land (grade 3) and hedgerows.

Land north of Wendbury is allocated for a new neighbourhood of about 950 homes, a two-form-entry primary school and a local centre. We expect the first homes to be completed in 2029 and the site to be finished by 2038.

Development must:

- provide a two-form-entry primary school on 2.1 hectares of serviced land, transferred to the County Council before the 300th home is occupied
- take vehicle access from a new roundabout on the Wend Valley Road, and provide walking and cycling routes to Wendbury station and the town centre
- contribute towards the improvement of Wendbury station and the frequent bus service to Harrowfield
- provide 35% affordable homes in line with Policy NW5
- manage surface water with sustainable drainage systems designed to the Wendshire SuDS guide
- retain the hedgerows and the Old Mill Lane footpath within a green corridor

### Policy NW/H2: Kettleford Road, Coldmarsh

Site reference: NW/H2. Existing use: paddocks and a former plant nursery.

Land at Kettleford Road, Coldmarsh is allocated for about 220 homes. Development should respect the character of Coldmarsh and its setting in the Fens.

### Policy NW/H3: Former Maltings, Wendbury

Site reference: NW/H3. Existing use: vacant maltings buildings, locally listed, and a surface car park.

The former Maltings is allocated for a mix of homes and town centre uses, to be delivered between 2031 and 2035. Development must:

- retain and reuse the malt kiln and the 1890s malting range
- be informed by a site-specific flood risk assessment and keep the ground floors of homes above the design flood level
- provide a new pedestrian route from the Market Place to the riverside

### Policy NW/E1: Wend Valley Business Park extension

Site reference: NW/E1. Site area: 18 hectares. Existing use: arable farmland.

Land east of the Wend Valley Business Park is allocated for about 60,000 square metres of employment floorspace for research and development, light industry, general industry and storage and distribution. Proposals should provide structural landscaping along the eastern boundary.

### Policy NW/GV1: Harrowfield Garden Village

Site reference: NW/GV1. Site area: 120 hectares. Existing use: the former RAF Harrowfield airfield, now partly used for storage, and farmland.

Harrowfield is allocated for a garden village of about 1,900 homes, 8 hectares of employment land, two primary schools, a secondary school, a health centre and a village centre. We expect the first homes to be completed in 2032 and about 1,500 homes to be completed by 2042, with the rest after the plan period.

Development must be in accordance with a masterplan approved by the Council, and must:

- provide a new junction on the Wend Valley Road before the 200th home is occupied
- fund a bus service every 15 minutes to Wendbury station from the first occupation
- provide the land for, and contribute to the cost of, the schools and the health centre
- fund the upgrade of the Wendbury water recycling centre needed to serve the site, agreed with Wendshire Water, before the 500th home is occupied
- provide at least 30 hectares of publicly accessible green space, including a country park on the eastern edge of the airfield

## 6 Development management policies

### Policy NW1: Sustainable development

When considering development proposals the Council will take a positive approach that reflects the presumption in favour of sustainable development contained in the National Planning Policy Framework. Planning applications that accord with the policies in this local plan will be approved without delay, unless material considerations indicate otherwise. Where there are no policies relevant to the application, or the most important policies for determining it are out of date, the Council will grant permission unless the adverse impacts of doing so would significantly and demonstrably outweigh the benefits, when assessed against the policies in the National Planning Policy Framework taken as a whole.

### Policy NW2: Design

All development must be of high quality design that is visually attractive, functional, sympathetic to local character and history, and establishes a strong sense of place.

In Wendbury town centre and the district's conservation areas, development must also follow the Northwold Design Code (2026), including its guidance on the Wolds vernacular of flint and soft red brick, and must not exceed the building heights set out in Appendix 3 of the code.

### Policy NW3: Heritage

Great weight will be given to the conservation of designated heritage assets. Proposals that would lead to substantial harm to, or total loss of significance of, a designated heritage asset will be refused unless it can be demonstrated that the substantial harm or loss is necessary to achieve substantial public benefits that outweigh that harm or loss. Where a proposal would lead to less than substantial harm, this harm will be weighed against the public benefits of the proposal.

### Policy NW4: Flood risk

Development in Flood Zones 2 and 3 must pass the sequential test and, where necessary, the exception test, as set out in national policy.

In the Wend valley, development must also:

- be set back at least 20 metres from the top of the bank of the River Wend, to allow access for maintenance and to make room for the river
- have finished floor levels at least 600 millimetres above the 1 in 100 year flood level, plus the allowance for climate change, shown in the Strategic Flood Risk Assessment
- not reduce the capacity of the Wendbury flood storage area shown on the policies map

### Policy NW5: Affordable housing

On sites of 10 or more homes, 35% of homes in Wendbury and Harrowfield, and between 20% and 30% of homes in Coldmarsh, Saxholme and the villages, must be affordable homes. Of the affordable homes, 60% should be for social rent and 40% for affordable home ownership, reflecting the Local Housing Needs Assessment (2025). The lower requirement outside Wendbury and Harrowfield reflects the findings of the Whole Plan Viability Assessment (2026).

### Policy NW6: Local Green Spaces and the Northwold Wolds

The fourteen spaces listed in Appendix 2 and shown on the policies map are designated as Local Green Spaces.

Within the Northwold Wolds, development must conserve and enhance the open chalk downland character of the hills and the views of them from the Wend valley identified in the Northwold Landscape Character Assessment, and must not break the skyline when seen from the viewpoints shown on the policies map.

### Policy NW7: Renewable energy

Proposals for renewable and low carbon energy development will be supported where their impacts are, or can be made, acceptable. Applicants will not be required to demonstrate the overall need for renewable or low carbon energy.

### Policy NW8: Town centres

Main town centre uses should be located in town centres, then in edge of centre locations, and only if suitable sites are not available should out of centre sites be considered.

An impact assessment will be required for retail and leisure proposals of more than 500 square metres outside the town centres of Wendbury, Coldmarsh and Saxholme. This threshold is lower than the national default because of the small size of these centres, as shown by the Retail and Town Centres Study (2025).

### Policy NW9: Net zero carbon homes

From 1 April 2030, all new homes must be net zero carbon in operation. New homes must achieve at least a 63% reduction in regulated carbon emissions compared with the Target Emission Rate of the 2021 Building Regulations, using on-site renewable energy, and must not use fossil fuels for heating or hot water.

### Policy NW10: Biodiversity

All development must deliver at least 10% biodiversity net gain, in accordance with the statutory requirement, and should do so on site where possible.

In the Wend valley and the Northwold Wolds, which are identified as priority areas in the Wendshire Local Nature Recovery Strategy (2025), major development must deliver at least 20% biodiversity net gain and contribute to the creation of the wetland and chalk grassland habitats the strategy maps there.

## 7 Delivery and viability

The Whole Plan Viability Assessment (2026) tested the policies in this plan together. It found that development in Wendbury and at Harrowfield can meet all of the plan's requirements, including 35% affordable housing and net zero carbon homes. Viability is more marginal in Coldmarsh, Saxholme and the villages, which is why Policy NW5 sets a lower requirement there.

The Council will work with landowners and developers to bring the allocated sites forward, and will consider using its compulsory purchase powers to assemble the Maltings site if necessary.
